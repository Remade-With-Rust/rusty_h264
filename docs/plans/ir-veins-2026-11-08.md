# Decoder instruction-count veins (2026-11-08)

Fifteen places to mine deterministic, byte-identical instruction reductions in the
H.264 decoder, ranked by measured return. Every number below is an EXACT executed
instruction count (callgrind `Ir`) from the shipping build (`asm` + `global-alloc`,
`target-cpu=x86-64-v3`, thin LTO), taken on one machine in one session. The clock was
not consulted: this box is permanently loaded, and every previous campaign here had
to work on static assembly counts or per-hypothesis counters. An executed count has
no variance, prices every function at once, and carries call counts and caller to
callee edges, which the static census cannot.

The campaign that mines these veins keeps its running total in
`docs/plans/ir-wins-ledger.md`.

(Restored 2026-11-08 after a concurrent encoder session overwrote this path; the
encoder's own plan is `docs/plans/ir-veins-encoder-2026-11-08.md`.)

### Three reading rules

1. **callgrind prices `rep stos`/`rep movs` per BYTE.** A 1 MB memset is ~1M Ir but
   ~50 us. The big per-picture clears (vein 4) are therefore over-priced in Ir relative
   to time; the ~1M TINY memset calls (vein 3) are not, since their cost is the call and
   the entry logic, not the bytes.
2. **A fast path is priced in calls, never in static instructions** (codec-measurement).
   Every vein here states the executed count it removes, and the static count is
   allowed to go up.
3. **Ir is blind to cache, branch misprediction and ILP.** A vein that is a layout
   change (14) will under-read here; nothing here is a cache claim. The converse
   trap: Ir REWARDS removing predictable branches (bounds checks, section 3) that cost
   the clock nothing. A brick whose only Ir win is in `slice/index.rs` lines is not a
   speed win.

## 1. The ranked veins

Return estimates are the share of total Ir the vein's mechanism accounts for on the
shape it hits hardest, and a conservative fraction of that share the fix removes.
Each vein names its gate: all are byte-identical (the 26-stream ffmpeg SHA gate in
`bench/ir_all.sh`) plus an unchanged `frames=` anchor.

### V1. Deblocking is 20-26% of all instructions, and more than half of it is orchestration

| stream | filter glue (self) | derivation (self, rows+records+edges) | SIMD kernels | pk_differs | total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FourPeople main | 5.4% | 4.2 + 2.4 + ~1.2% | 6.0% | 3.4% | ~23% |
| shields cavlc | 8.3% | 2.0 + 1.2 + 0.8% | 13.5% | 0 | ~26% |
| shields high | 5.3% | 2.5 + 0.9 + ~0.8% | 7.0% | 1.0% | ~18% |

`filter_frame_rows_pre` SELF (no kernel inside) is 23k-73k Ir per macroblock ROW:
290-909 Ir per macroblock of edge loop, mask tests, threshold derivation and kernel
argument marshalling, against 2.3 + 2.3 luma and 1.2 + 1.1 chroma kernel calls per MB.
That is ~130 Ir of glue per filtered edge. The kernels are called per EDGE with the
four lane strengths and three thresholds as separate arguments.

Fix shape (openh264's `DeblockingMbLuma`): one entry per macroblock that takes the
packed `MbBs` record (already u8) and filters all four vertical then all four
horizontal luma edges and both chroma planes, deriving alpha/beta/tc0 ONCE per MB for
the internal edges (they share the MB's QP) and once per MB edge. The mask tests
collapse to one `u32` per edge group (already computed as `vnz`/`hnz`). Kernel argument
marshalling disappears into one call.

Expected: 3-5% of Ir on every shape. Owner functions: `deblock.rs filter_frame_rows_impl`,
`deblock.rs derive_mb_records`, `mb16.rs derive_bs_row`.

### V2. The motion-compensation ENTRY path costs as much as the MC kernels

Self cost of the four glue functions around the MC kernels (the kernels themselves are
separate symbols and are NOT included):

| stream | `mc_luma_padded_pre` | `mc_chroma_padded_pair` | `b_mc` | `b_mc_chroma` | glue total | MC kernels |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| foreman main | 1.8% (145/call) | 2.0% (156/call) | 2.6% (360/call) | 2.4% (321/call) | **8.8%** | ~9% |
| FourPeople high | 5.2% (217/call) | 5.2% (218/call) | 7.9% (662/call) | 7.0% (582/call) | **25.3%** | ~6% |
| shields high | 1.9% | 2.1% | 2.8% | 2.4% | **9.2%** | ~9% |

This is the rusty_h265 finding exactly: the kernels were finished and the entry path
was not. Per call `mc_luma_padded_pre` recomputes `intact` (two multiplies, two
compares against plane geometry that is a per-PICTURE invariant of the reference),
the five-term `in_range`, the halo address, and dispatches; `mc_chroma_padded_pair`
does the same again for chroma. `b_mc` clamps both reference indices against
`refs.len()`, probes `refs.get()`, re-reads `luma_guard()`, calls `implicit_weights`
(202k calls, 81 Ir each on FourPeople high: 1.0% on its own) and tests the bi/uni/full
shape per region; `b_mc_chroma` repeats the shape dispatch.

Fix shape: resolve a per-slice reference plan once (planes validated, strides, pads,
implicit-weight TABLE indexed by (refi0, refi1), both lists clamped); one combined
in-range test per partition covering luma and chroma; the full/uni/bi shape decided
once per region. Expected: 3-6% of Ir on B content, 1-2% on CAVLC. Owner: `inter.rs
mc_luma_padded_pre`, `inter.rs mc_chroma_padded_pair`, `mb16.rs b_mc`, `b_mc_chroma`,
`implicit_weights`.

### V3. A million tiny `fill`s are a million `memset` calls (a prior brick reversed by the exact counter)

| stream | memset calls from `b_set_motion` | `commit_inter_grid` | `decode_b_direct` | `bz/pz_flush_slow` | Ir in those calls | caller self Ir |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| foreman main | 763,500 | 153,324 | - | 14,991 | 15.0M (2.4%) | 24.5M + 3.4M |
| FourPeople main | 849,060 | 215,892 | - | 468,936 | 23.1M (2.0%) | 26.9M + 4.8M |
| shields high | 2,499,780 | 876,858 | 470,040 | 126,000 | 63.4M (2.1%) | 79.1M + ~10M |

`b_set_motion` writes seven grids per block row with `fill` over a runtime-length slice
of 1-4 elements. LLVM lowers each to a libc `memset` call: 15 Ir per call for 1-4 bytes
of payload, 28 calls per 16x16 region. `commit_inter_grid`, the band flushes and
`decode_b_direct`'s per-sub-block writes have the same shape. The dec-mb-P round of
2026-08-21 introduced several of these "row fills" judged on the STATIC count, which
fell; the executed count rose, which only this instrument can see.

Fix shape: `rw` is one of 16/8/4, so `bw` is 4/2/1. Dispatch on it and fill
CONSTANT-length slices (`&mut x[a..][..N]`), which LLVM turns into stores. Expected: 3-4%
of Ir on B/P content.

### V4. Per-picture and per-slice clears plus zeroed output planes: ~14% of Ir on LIGHT content

| memset source (FourPeople main) | calls | Ir | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `rusty_alloc` zeroing fresh allocations (`vec![0; cw*ch]` output planes) | 244 | 88.9M | 7.7% |
| `with_pool` grid refill (18 grids per picture) | 1,080 | 51.2M | 4.4% |
| `decode_slice_cabac_inner` per-slice scratch refill (up to 11 grids) | 1,023 | 24.6M | 2.1% |

Read rule 1 applies: these are byte-priced, so in TIME this is ~2.7 MB of memset per
720p picture, ~200-270 us against ~3.7 ms of decode on this clip, i.e. 5-7% of time
on LIGHT content and ~2% on dense.

* **Grids every macroblock rewrites do not need clearing.** Keep the pooled Vec at full
  length and skip the `resize` for any grid that every macroblock kind writes before
  any read. **Gate by POISON, not by argument:** fill the grid with garbage under a
  temporary switch; if the 26-stream gate still passes the clear was dead on the
  corpus, and code reading then has to cover the paths the corpus does not reach.
* **Output planes.** `rec_y/u/v` are fresh zeroed allocations per picture that every
  pixel overwrites. Safe Rust cannot hand out uninitialised memory, so the lever is an
  API: a caller returns finished frames to a plane pool.
* **Per-slice scratch.** The eleven `sc_*` grids are refilled per SLICE over the whole
  picture.
* **Reference padding writes every row three times** (`pad_plane_into`: memset the left
  pad, memcpy the interior, memset the right pad; 1.2% of FourPeople main).

Expected: 3-6% of Ir on LIGHT content (and a real time win, unlike most of this list).

### V5. The B_Skip band average calls a 16-byte kernel per 16 bytes

`avg_row_into` is 6.45% of FourPeople main (474,064 calls, 158 Ir each) and 2.8% of
FourPeople high. The zero-bi band recon calls it once per pixel ROW of the band;
inside, it walks the row in 16-byte pieces, each piece a separate call into the accel
`pixel_avg` kernel with height 1. One `vpavgb ymm` averages 32 bytes in one
instruction. Fix: one call per row or per band; LLVM matches the safe
`((a as u16 + b as u16 + 1) >> 1) as u8` loop to `vpavgb` itself. Expected: -6% of Ir
on static B content, ~0 elsewhere.

### V7. The inter residual ladder is 15% of CAVLC detail content

`add_inter_residual` self: 355M Ir = 15.3% on shields cavlc (2,507 Ir per call), 205M =
6.9% on shields high, 5.9% foreman main. Candidates: the per-block `nnz_y` scatter (16
bounds-checked stores per MB into a strided grid), the per-block offset arithmetic and
three-way ladder branch, the chroma `nnz_c` write per block, the 4-byte zero-residual
row copies (line census: `mb16.rs` zero-residual copy lines 0.86% of shields cavlc),
and coefficients carried as `i32`. Expected: 2-4% on detail content.

### V8. The slice loop's own body is 10-11% of Ir, unattributed

`decode_slice_cabac_inner` self: 10.4% foreman main (1,340 Ir/MB), 11.5% FourPeople main
(620 Ir/MB), 10.0% shields high (1,380 Ir/MB); `decode_slice_cavlc_inner` 4.5% shields
cavlc. Every residual parser, MC glue and recon function is a separate symbol, so this
is PURE orchestration: bins, the two 30-entry `mvdc`/`refc` neighbour caches built per
inter MB, sub-MB-type and intra-mode parsing, span and EDC bookkeeping, `row_hook_at` +
`wait_refs_for_mb` + `span_flush` per MB. Line census: 2.3-3.1% of Ir is compiler-
generated code with no source line (prologue, spills, reloads) attributed to `mb16.rs`.
Expected: 2-3% of Ir.

## 3. Line census (line-tables binary, `_ir/cg_dbg/`)

Rust inlines so aggressively that most self cost lands on std's source lines
(`uint_macros.rs`, `cmp.rs`, `slice/index.rs`, `ptr/mod.rs`, `core_arch`): read those
as integer ops, min/max/clamp, bounds checks, pointer loads/stores and SIMD intrinsics
inlined into OUR functions, not as std being slow.

| std-attributed bucket | FourPeople main | shields cavlc | shields high | reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `slice/index.rs` (executed bounds-check code) | ~3.5% | 2.8% | 2.1% | the bounds-check tax in INSTRUCTIONS; ~0 in TIME per the ledger's `get_unchecked` probe |
| `mb16.rs:0` (no source line: prologue, spills, reloads) | 2.3% | 1.3% | 3.1% | the frame-economics half of V8 |
| `cmp.rs` (min/max/clamp) | 1.4% | 2.7% | 3.5% | clamps on the hot path |
| `iter/range.rs` (loop counters) | 1.0% | 2.4% | 2.4% | counted loops |
| `cabac.rs` decision-bin lines | 1.3% | - | 3.4% | the bin engine, at its floor |

Decoder-owned lines it names: `avg_row_into`'s kernel-call loop (1.17% FourPeople main,
V5); the zero-residual 4-byte row copies in `add_inter_residual` (0.86% + 0.38% shields
cavlc, V6/V7); `Cursor::peek24`, cursor commit, `peek_bits`, `lut_read`, `nnz_cache_set`
(~2.4% shields cavlc, V9); `un_scan_8x8` (0.75%) and `inverse_core_8x8` (1.26%) on shields

The line-tables binary is a different build (total Ir within +4k of the shipping
binary on two streams, -266k on the third): attribution only, never a verdict.

## 4. How a brick is run

1. `bash bench/ir_step.sh <tag> <prev>` builds, gates 26 streams on ffmpeg's SHA, counts
   seven, prints deltas. Anchors identical or the run aborts.
2. Probe before building where the prize is uncertain.
3. One change per measurement; `bench/ir_cmp.py --fn` attributes what moved.
4. The repo test suite and the scalar arm at vein boundaries.
5. A refuted brick is reverted and its number recorded in the ledger.
6. After any brick that moves an inlining boundary, re-test the largest refutations.

## 5. Not to be re-tried (refuted elsewhere in the ledger)

* A full scan8 MV cache (1.13x SLOWER, 11/11), a whole-MB direct memo (7/7 slower),
  `get_unchecked` (flat), MB-local pixel tiles (not cache-bound), a batched
  lane-per-block IDCT, the CABAC engine itself, row-slicing `weight_partition`'s inner
  loops (+28% instructions), packing the deblock `[bool;4]` masks into bits.

## 6. Correctness defect found by the feature corpus (fixed 2026-11-08)

`disable_deblocking_filter_idc == 1` (x264 `--no-deblock`) left a skip run at the END of
a picture unreconstructed: the pending P/B skip spans and queued pixel jobs were flushed
only inside `FrameDecoder::deblock()`, which the finalize skips when the filter is off.
Symptom on `_ir/feat/nodeblock.264`: the last two macroblocks of the last B picture
differed from ffmpeg by up to 190. Fix: `FrameDecoder::finish_pending()`, called by the
finalize unconditionally before the filter. The feature corpus now gates it.
