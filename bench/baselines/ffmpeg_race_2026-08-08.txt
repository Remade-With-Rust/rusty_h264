WHAT SITS BETWEEN 0.8.0 AND THIS RUN: the Great Gate decoder work (E2/E3
slice-worker threading, e2-seam-dispatch, CAVLC recon convergence, the CAVLC
E-seam) AND the rip-ASM campaign (81% of the vendored NASM replaced with
portable Rust SIMD: chroma MC, luma MC, deblock, SATD/SAD).

WHY CAVLC IS THE ODD ONE OUT, structurally: `edc_dispatch` requires `cabac`,
so the campaign's flagship decoder gate is INERT on CAVLC by construction (the
clause exists because the rule scored net -52.30 / worst class -40.85 once
CAVLC entered the corpus). main and high therefore received a speedup that
could offset any port cost; cavlc received none of it.

AND A METHODOLOGY FAILURE TO OWN: rip-ASM Phases 2 and 3 were validated almost
entirely on `__main` streams. cavlc was under-sampled, and cavlc is the tier
that moved. That is the "gate arms must cross axes" law, broken in the very
work that cites it. Any future kernel port must measure cavlc/main/high.

NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the port owns the cavlc move. Two localisation
attempts failed on this box -- the first to a PARSER BUG (`[\d,]+\s*ms` matched
the "1" in ".1ms", returning ~1.0 every run; the tell was a 100-250% noise
floor, which is impossible for identical-work arms), the second to genuine
machine instability (null-arm floor 81%). Re-run on a quiet box with
best-of-N INSIDE the process.
